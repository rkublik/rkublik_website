Too many documents

We had a government client (we'll call them TLA - Three Letter Acronym) who was a member of a large multinational organization, we'll call Big Org. TLA wanted to exert more influence within Big Org, and needed to understand how the politics and processes affected the final decisions.

We had access to Big Org's data repository, containing over 2 terabytes of text documents and growing. It was a challenge to make sense of such a large quantity of text data that was not indexed or organized.

I led a team of 5 engineers and data scientists to collect, analyze, and explain the data to TLA. We developed tools and an automated pipeline to identify and download new data from the repository, extract metadata and store it in a database to make the data accessible. We analyzed the metadata to identify activity trends, and with TLA's input we dug deeper. We used machine learning models to perform topic analysis and sentiment analysis, and created interactive data visualizations to help the client explore the data.

At the onset of the project, TLA suspected a group within Big Org was having outsized influence over the decision making process. We were able to find evidence supporting that claim, and identified some of the ways it was happening.

I enjoyed this project, and the opportunity to focus more on the high-level goals and ideas. As with every project, I learned a lot, and gained technical skills and domain knowledge in a new area, skills in leading a cross-functional team, and improved my presentation skills to share our work with the client. It was gratifying to see the junior members of my team grow their own abilities and contribute their ideas and skills across the project.
